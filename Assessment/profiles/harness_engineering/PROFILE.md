# Harness Engineering assessment profile

This profile assesses what an individual learner demonstrates while designing, using, or revising a harness for AI-assisted work. Bind it to the supplied task brief, taught knowledge, and assignment conditions before assessment.

## Constructs

| Construct | Operational definition | Admissible evidence |
|---|---|---|
| K — Applied Domain Knowledge | Correct application of the taught task knowledge or method | A concrete task, learner application, and inspectable result consistent with the teaching requirements |
| M — Hybrid Metacognitive Regulation | Planning, monitoring, evaluation/calibration, and adaptive regulation | A learner-attributed proactive or reactive episode that meets the relevant M1–M4 definition |
| R — Conditional Professional Judgment | A decision linked to conditions, rationale/mechanism, and a boundary, exception, or revision trigger | An attributable task-specific choice whose reasoning and limits can be recovered from the evidence |
| I — Prospective Insight | Current observation connected through a mechanism to a transferable principle and future test or boundary condition | An attributable reasoning chain grounded in the current task evidence |

Apply the [construct rules](construct_rules.md) for indicators, counterexamples, and episode coding. M1–M4 retain their definitions across tasks.

## Task knowledge

Before inspecting learner evidence, identify the actual task, its taught knowledge points, required demonstrations, permitted assistance, starter prompts, and verification conditions. The supplied brief and teaching sources define relevant knowledge and scope. Record missing inputs and the resulting uncertainty. If task knowledge cannot be identified, request that information before assessing K.

## Evidence thresholds and attribution

Apply the [evidence model](../../session_evidence/references/evidence_model.md) and [episode/attribution rules](../../session_evidence/references/episode_and_attribution_rules.md).

Task-specific learner application can support K even when scaffolded; record its degree of independence. Agent-created artifacts establish an output claim until learner selection, evaluation, adaptation, or use is visible.

M requires visible learner regulation. A failed command, an Agent-authored plan, repeated retries, or unexamined acceptance does not by itself establish M. Record each episode's goal, signal or anticipated risk, learner response, change, follow-up, and observation opportunity when present.

R requires a conditional design decision with a rationale and boundary. Compliance alone leaves the judgment unestablished. I requires the observation–mechanism–transfer–future-test chain; a list of future tools or recommendations alone is insufficient.

Analyze relevant sessions first. Inspect artifacts and reflections independently before selecting which constructs they inform. Preserve copied scaffolds, derivative reports, source contradictions, partial records, and supplementary-only findings. A retrospective explanation can clarify a judgment without proving contemporaneous regulation.

## Reporting

Report K, M1–M4, R, and I separately, using the [report contract](../../session_evidence/references/report_contract.md). For each construct include evidence strength, confidence, direct locators, learner attribution, opportunity/coverage, dependence, contradictions, and limitations.

Use the evidence-strength labels defined by that contract. Distinguish insufficient evidence from no preserved observation opportunity. When a separately supplied rubric requests numeric coursework scores, retain its item codes, anchors, and missing-evidence rules and show those scores separately from learner-ability claims.

Report AI-augmented task performance (`P^AI`) separately from K/M/R/I. A verified successful artifact does not alone establish learner understanding; an unsuccessful or blocked task may still contain strong regulation or judgment evidence. Keep the construct vectors separate throughout the report.
